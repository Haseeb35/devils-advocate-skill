# Personal: quitting a job to freelance

> **Prompt:** should I really quit my job as a frontend developer to go freelance? I have 3 months of savings and one client who said they'd probably have work for me.

<sub>Illustrative example written to show the format and tone for this kind of decision.</sub>

## 😈 Devil's Advocate

**The plan:** Leave a salaried frontend role to freelance full-time, with three months of savings and one likely client.

**The case against**
"Probably have work" is not a contract, and one client is a single point of failure. Freelance income typically takes three to six months to stabilise — sales cycles, late invoices and gaps between projects all eat into savings faster than expected. Three months of runway leaves almost no margin if the first client slips.

**3 ways this could fail**
1. **The anchor client falls through** — budgets change, and "probably" becomes "next quarter".
   Likelihood: Medium · Warning sign: no signed agreement or start date before your notice period ends · Prevent it: get a written scope and deposit before you resign.
2. **Runway runs out before the pipeline fills** — finding the second and third clients takes longer than planned.
   Likelihood: High · Warning sign: fewer than two active leads by week four · Prevent it: start building a pipeline now, and aim for six months of savings before leaving.
3. **Hidden costs shrink the income** — taxes, unpaid admin time, equipment and no paid leave.
   Likelihood: Medium · Warning sign: your effective hourly rate falls below your current salary equivalent · Prevent it: set your rate at roughly 1.5–2× your salaried hourly rate.

**The case for**
Frontend work is well suited to freelancing, you already have a warm lead, and an early career move like this is easier to reverse than a later one — if it doesn't work out, a solid developer can usually return to a full-time role.

**Verdict: ⚠️ Go, with changes**
The goal is reasonable; the timing isn't yet. Line up a signed first project and build toward six months of runway — then leave with a pipeline instead of a hope.

**Before you decide:** If the first client disappeared tomorrow, how many months could you go without income?

*It's your call, and it's a bigger one than any technical decision. Taking a few extra months to de-risk it doesn't mean you're not ready.*
