# Business: switching to subscription pricing

> **Prompt:** devil's advocate — we sell a one-time $49 desktop app with about 2,000 sales a year. I want to switch to $8/month subscription only, starting next month.

<sub>Illustrative example written to show the format and tone for this kind of decision.</sub>

## 😈 Devil's Advocate

**The plan:** Replace the $49 one-time license with an $8/month subscription for all new customers, starting next month.

**The case against**
Your customers chose a one-time purchase, and desktop-app buyers are among the most subscription-averse audiences there are. On paper $8/month beats $49 after six months — but only if people sign up at the same rate and stay. A sudden switch with a month's notice risks a sharp drop in conversions and a wave of public backlash that outlasts the change itself.

**3 ways this could fail**
1. **Conversions collapse** — visitors who would have paid $49 once won't commit to a recurring charge.
   Likelihood: High · Warning sign: trial-to-paid rate drops more than 30% in the first month · Prevent it: keep the one-time option at a higher price ($79–99) alongside the subscription.
2. **Churn erases the math** — subscribers cancel after two or three months, earning you less than the old $49.
   Likelihood: Medium · Warning sign: month-2 retention under 70% · Prevent it: offer an annual plan at a discount and ship visible updates monthly so the subscription feels earned.
3. **Existing users revolt** — current owners assume they'll be forced over and leave angry reviews.
   Likelihood: Medium · Warning sign: support tickets and forum threads about "rug pulls" · Prevent it: announce clearly that existing licenses stay valid forever.

**The case for**
Recurring revenue makes the business far more predictable and funds ongoing development. If you already ship regular updates and have users who'd pay for cloud sync or similar ongoing value, a subscription matches how the product is actually used.

**Verdict: ⚠️ Go, with changes**
Add the subscription, don't replace the license yet. Run both for a quarter, then decide based on real revenue per visitor rather than projections.

**Before you decide:** What do subscribers get every month that one-time buyers wouldn't?
